Axiom — Neural RLHF

A 24-hour hackathon project that helped three AI agents adapt to a person’s focus and engagement, measured with an EEG headband.

View the team repository

Overview

Axiom asks whether an AI system can learn from a person’s response without requiring them to speak, type, or press a rating button. Three AI agents explain the same topic in different styles while an EEG headband measures broad signs of focus and engagement.

  • My role: co-creator; proposed the assistive-communication concept and improved the timing between calibration, live readings, and the AI system.
  • Team build: Muse S headband, Python signal processing, three AI personas, and a React interface.
  • Format: completed as a 24-hour hackathon prototype.

How it works

01Agents answerThree styles respond to the same topic.
→
02User readsEach response appears for a timed window.
→
03EEG measuresThe headband tracks broad changes in engagement.
→
04System scoresResponses receive a neural feedback score.
→
05Agents adaptThe next round leans toward the preferred style.

RLHF, in plain English: improving an AI system using feedback from people. In this prototype, an EEG-based score replaces the usual thumbs-up or written rating.

My contribution

I proposed the original idea as an assistive interface for people who cannot easily rely on speech or manual input. During the build, I worked on startup synchronization: the system first records the user’s normal baseline, then accepts only fresh EEG readings from the current round.

Without that timing control, an agent could be scored using noise or data left over from the previous response. Aligning the interface, EEG stream, and scoring window made the demo’s feedback more trustworthy.

Main challenge

EEG signals are noisy and can change with movement or poor sensor contact. We treated the result as an experimental preference signal, not a direct reading of intent. A simulation mode also let the team test the full software flow when the physical headband was unavailable or unreliable.

Just as importantly, this was a team project. I shaped the concept and timing layer; teammates built major parts of the signal-processing and agent system.

Result

The prototype completed repeated rounds: calibrate, present three responses, record feedback, choose a winner, and adjust the agents. It demonstrated the interaction, but it did not prove that EEG can reliably replace deliberate user ratings in everyday use.

The same hackathon also produced Apollo 1, which used the focus signal to control a rover instead of adapting text.
