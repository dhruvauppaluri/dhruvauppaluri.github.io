DIY Light Meter — Film & Mirrorless Cameras

A handheld meter that recommends camera settings from the available light.

Overview

I shoot film and use manual lenses, so I wanted an affordable meter that works independently of the camera. This prototype measures a scene and recommends a practical exposure.

  • My role: solo circuit, firmware, interface, and calibration.
  • Parts: Arduino Uno, light-sensitive resistor, small display, and rotary control.
  • Two modes: film mode keeps the roll’s ISO fixed; mirrorless mode can raise ISO to keep the shutter speed usable.
  • Status: working breadboard prototype; a portable enclosure is next.

What I built

The Arduino converts the light reading into exposure value, combines it with the chosen aperture and ISO, and selects the nearest standard shutter speed. The rotary control edits settings and switches between film and mirrorless modes.

I calibrated the inexpensive sensor against a reference meter in indoor light, shade, overcast weather, and direct sun. The readings stayed within roughly one-third of a stop in those tests.

Main challenge

The rotary control originally missed turns because the program paused for display updates. I removed the blocking delay and let input, sensing, and display updates run on separate timers. The interface then responded immediately.

A second problem came from using Arduino pin 13, whose built-in LED affected the input signal. Moving the connection to pin 12 restored reliable direction detection.

Result and next step

The breadboard version works in both modes. Next I want to replace the approximate light sensor with a calibrated digital sensor, design a compact circuit board, and build an enclosure suitable for carrying with a camera.
