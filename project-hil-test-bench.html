Steering-Actuator Latency Test Bench

A test bench that checks whether my RC-car steering parts respond quickly enough for real-time correction.

Overview

I want to build an RC car that can notice a slide and correct its steering. Before designing that controller, I needed to know whether the basic parts could react quickly enough.

  • My role: solo hardware, firmware, and data analysis.
  • Parts: joystick, Arduino Uno, steering servo, and MPU6050 motion sensor.
  • Question: does real motion begin within 50 milliseconds of a steering command?
  • Answer: yes—the measured response stayed under that target.

How it works

01Move joystickCreate a steering command.
→
02Arduino commandsConvert the input into a servo angle.
→
03Servo turnsProduce real mechanical movement.
→
04Sensor detectsThe gyroscope measures the response.
→
05Computer comparesMeasure time from command to motion.

Hardware-in-the-loop, in plain English: testing real electronic and mechanical parts while software measures how they behave.

Making the data usable

The motion sensor’s raw signal was noisy, so the start of movement was hard to identify. I first measured its resting offset, then used a lightweight moving average to smooth random spikes without hiding the real response.

The balance mattered: too much smoothing would make the system look slower than it was, while too little would make noise look like motion.

Test bench

Result and next step

The measured end-to-end delay was under 50 milliseconds, so these parts are fast enough for the next stage of the RC-car project.

This bench does not correct a slide by itself. The next step is closing the loop: using the measured motion to adjust steering automatically while the car is moving.
